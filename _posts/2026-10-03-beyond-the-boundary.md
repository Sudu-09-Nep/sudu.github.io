It had been only a week since Squee and Fewee had started exploring the world. Every morning they followed the same routine, leaving the warm hollow of their coconut tree to search for food and adventure among the branches. Such a nice life, nothing to worry about, just eat, sleep, and repeat.

The tall coconut trees looked like endless ladders reaching the sky. Squee and Fewee would race up and down the rough trunks, their tiny claws gripping the bark as if the trees were built just for their games.

Sometimes others would peek from nearby branches, watching the two little adventurers chase each other. After a moment of curiosity, they would join in too, turning the vertical trees into a lively playground of running tails and squeaking laughter.

Next to the coconut garden stood a mango grove. The trees there were older and thicker, their branches spreading wide and casting deep shadows on the ground. During summer, the sweet aroma from the grove filled the entire air.

Sometimes, from the very top of a coconut tree, Squee would pause and stare at the mysterious mango forest. From far away it looked dark and quiet, almost like a secret world hidden beneath the canopy.

He often wondered what was inside that shaded grove.

From their earliest days, their mother had taught them the habits of safe survival. During their first excursions outside the nest, she would lead them early in the morning through the nearby trees in search of food.

Whenever they found something to eat, they would nibble it right there on the branch. But if they sensed danger, a sudden rustle of leaves or the shadow of a bird above, they would quickly climb to a higher, safer branch and finish their meal there.

Their mother had slowly planted confidence in both of them, teaching them when to be brave and when to be careful.

"Always watch the sky and the ground before you move," their mother would say. "The danger you cannot see is often the one that catches you."

Today, as usual, they left the nest with their mother. It was a lovely morning, and the three of them roamed around the coconut garden, leaping from trunk to trunk. Their mother led them to places they had never explored before, places just adjacent to the mango grove. Squee was more than excited.

The sky was painted with gentle gradients of orange as the sun slowly rose above the trees. From the nearby mango grove, a sweet aroma drifted through the air, filling the garden with the scent of ripening fruit. The whole forest seemed alive with a quiet excitement, as if nature itself were waiting for the day to begin.

After the morning hunt, they went into the shade to rest. The mother looked at her children and warned them strictly not to cross the boundary. Then she settled down and soon drifted into sleep.

No sooner had their mother closed her eyes than curiosity began to tickle Squee. He looked around, his little eyes shining with excitement. The branches of the mango trees swayed gently in the breeze, inviting him to explore.

In the next moment, there was Squee jumping from one branch to another in the mango grove, his bushy tail flicking with delight as he discovered the world beyond the boundary his mother had forbidden him to cross.

The new territory felt wonderful to him. For a moment he even imagined that one day this place might become his own. As he looked around, all he could see were clusters of yellow mangoes hanging from the branches, their sweet aroma filling the warm air of the grove.

Squee felt proud of his discovery.

Fewee will never believe this, he thought. I must show her.

So he scampered along a branch, plucked a ripe mango, and nibbled half of it. Then, holding the remaining piece carefully in his mouth, he hurried back toward the place where his sister was resting.

Meanwhile, in the mango grove, a flock of birds suddenly burst into the air, their wings beating loudly among the branches.

But Squee was too excited to notice. The thrill of discovering the new territory had blinded his ability to sense danger. He continued to scamper along the branches, unaware that something in the grove had disturbed the quiet morning.

Squee quietly returned to where his sister was resting. Fewee was sleeping peacefully at the fork of a branch, her bushy tail gently covering her small face, her two big shiny black eyes hidden behind long brown, black, and white lashes. A few metres away, their mother was also sleeping on another branch, resting after the morning's search for food.

Squee and Fewee were almost always together. But this time Squee felt something different. He had discovered something new, something exciting, all by himself.

Carefully, he placed the half-eaten mango beside her and gently nudged her awake.

"Fewee… wake up," he whispered softly.

"What happened?" Fewee retorted, irritated.

With a victorious smile on his face, Squee pointed proudly at the mango lying beside her.

"What…?!" Fewee whispered in surprise.

"Shh… Mother will wake up," Squee said softly.

"Where did you get this?" Fewee asked, her beautiful black eyes widening.

Squee puffed up with pride.

"From my territory," he said.

"Your territory?" Fewee frowned.

"Yes," Squee replied. "It's a wonderful place. Do you want to see it?"

Before Fewee could say anything more, Squee had already leapt onto the next branch.

"Wait!" Fewee whispered urgently as she hurried after him.

Squee ran with all the speed his little body could manage. He did not look at the sky, nor did he look down at the branches beneath his feet. His mother's warning had completely slipped from his mind.

Behind him, Fewee struggled to keep up.

In a few quick leaps, Squee crossed the boundary their mother had strictly forbidden them to cross.

Fewee stopped abruptly at the edge of the coconut garden. Her tiny claws gripped the bark of the branch as she stared at the invisible line in front of her.

"Brother, stop! Don't go there!" she called in a trembling voice.

But Squee did not hear her.

Fewee's heart began to pound. She stood there at the very edge, frozen between two choices.

If she crossed the boundary, she would be disobeying their mother. But if she stayed back, Squee would go alone into a place they had been warned about again and again.

Her mind raced.

Should I follow him? What if Mother wakes up? What if something happens to him? What is my duty?

For the first time in her small life, Fewee felt a sudden crisis inside her heart. Part of her wanted to run back and tell their mother that Squee had crossed the forbidden line. That would be the safe thing to do.

But another voice inside her would not let her move.

She wanted her brother to come back.

After all, he had come to wake her because he wanted to share something beautiful with her. His excitement, his joy, and his love had brought her here. The branches around her swayed gently in the warm breeze, but to Fewee the whole forest suddenly felt heavy and silent.

"Squee… Squee… where are you? It is time to go home. We are leaving," she called out, her voice rising.

She waited for a response from her brother, but nothing came back except silence.

Mother had warned us not to come here… but this fellow… muttered Fewee, panic slowly climbing into her voice.

Just then, a slight movement on a nearby mango tree caught her attention.

She turned her head slowly.

There, stretched along a thick branch, was a long, heavy creature sliding silently over the bark.

A snake……….

Fewee's heart nearly stopped. And to her horror, she saw Squee running straight toward that very branch, completely blinded by his excitement.

Fewee could no longer stand frozen. She burst into a loud, rapid chip–chip–chip call — the sharp alarm when danger appears. She flicked her bushy tail again and again, waving it wildly to draw the predator's attention away from her brother.

The snake lifted its head slightly.

At that very moment, Squee heard Fewee's urgent call. Instantly his excitement vanished. His body stiffened, and he froze on the branch.

Then he saw it.

Just a few metres ahead of him, stretched across the mango branch, was the snake.

Meanwhile, Fewee continued her desperate performance, darting along the branch, flicking her tail, and calling loudly. Her only thought was to pull the snake's attention away from Squee.

The alarm calls quickly spread through the garden. Hearing Fewee's sharp cries, other squirrels appeared from nearby trees. They gathered on the branches, chattering loudly at the dangerous scene unfolding before them.

When they saw Squee and the snake facing each other, many of them thought it might be the young squirrel's last day.

But the snake had now noticed Fewee's frantic movements. Her large, flicking tail caught its attention.

Slowly, the snake began to move toward her.

That single moment was enough.

Squee sprang into action. He leapt across the branches with all the strength his small body could gather. The snake suddenly turned, confused, unsure whether to chase Fewee or the fleeing Squee.

Squee raced through the branches, jumping from one limb to another. Behind him, the snake slithered in pursuit, but Squee was faster.

It was a thrilling sight, the small squirrel twisting, leaping, and dodging through the trees, escaping the predator again and again. At last, Squee made one final leap across the boundary back into the coconut garden.

He was safe.

Fewee watched in disbelief. For a moment she could hardly breathe. Then joy burst through her like sunlight after a storm.

She ran toward him, her tail dancing with excitement.

Squee was alive. And Fewee's happiness knew no bounds.

For a long moment, the coconut garden echoed with excited chatter. Other squirrels, who had gathered during the alarm call, now relaxed on the branches. Some flicked their tails in relief. Others gave soft warning chirps toward the mango grove, reminding everyone that the snake was still somewhere among the shadows.

But Squee and Fewee heard none of it.

Fewee rushed to her brother and pressed close to him, her small body trembling slightly.

"You foolish idiot," she said breathlessly. "Mother warned us not to cross that place."

Squee lowered his head a little.

For the first time since morning, he remembered his mother's words.

The danger you cannot see is often the one that catches you.

He looked back toward the dark mango grove. The branches swayed gently, hiding the silent world that had almost become his last.

"I'm sorry," he said quietly.

Fewee flicked her tail.

"You should be."

For a moment they both sat silently on the branch, feeling the warm breeze of the afternoon.

Then Fewee suddenly noticed something. The half-eaten mango that Squee had carried earlier was still beside him.

She stared at it.

"So this is the great treasure of your territory?" she said.

Squee looked down at the fruit and laughed softly. "Yes."

He placed it beside her. Fewee sniffed it carefully, then took a small bite. The juice was sweet and warm from the sun.

Her eyes widened. "It is good," she admitted.

The two siblings sat together, quietly finishing the mango. Above them, the coconut leaves rustled gently in the wind. The danger had passed, and the forest slowly returned to its peaceful rhythm.

But high in the branches of a distant mango tree, someone had been watching everything.

A young squirrel named Mira had seen the entire scene, the snake, Fewee's brave distraction, and Squee's daring escape through the branches. Her heart had raced during the chase. She had never seen such quick leaps or such fearless movement through the trees.

Now she watched the two siblings sharing the mango.

Squee looked different from the other young squirrels she had seen before. There was something bold about him, something restless and alive.

Mira flicked her tail thoughtfully.

"Maybe," she whispered to herself, "I should meet him someday."

With that quiet thought, she disappeared among the leaves.

Days passed in the coconut garden. The summer grew warmer, and the mangoes ripened in golden clusters across the grove. Life returned to its normal rhythm, running along trunks, leaping across branches, and gathering food beneath the bright sky.

One afternoon, when the wind moved softly through the trees, Squee noticed a new squirrel on a nearby branch. She watched him with curious eyes. Her fur was smooth and bright. Suddenly he saw a lot of butterflies, black and white, some dark and bright flying around him. For a moment he felt he was dreaming. But Fewee's movement brought him back to the realm. She moved forward to welcome the new stranger.

Fewee tilted her head. "Who are you?" she asked.

"My name is Mira," she replied.

Squee, who was resting nearby, watched the two quietly with interest.

Soon the young squirrels began to run together along the branches. They chased each other through the trees, shared fruits, and explored the edges of the garden. Days turned into weeks. And slowly, something deeper began to grow.

Mira would come to their nest early in the morning. She would sit on the nearby branch and call softly until Squee and Fewee woke up. Soon the three of them would be racing through the branches together, playing and exploring every corner of the coconut garden.

Sometimes they chased each other up the tall trunks, their tiny claws scratching softly against the bark. At other times they simply sat on a branch, sharing fruits and watching the world below. Those mornings were filled with laughter, movement, and the simple happiness that only young creatures can feel.

Mira even took them to her own nest in the mango tree that stood right at the boundary of the garden. From there they could see both worlds, the familiar coconut grove and the darker mango forest beyond. To Squee it felt like standing at the doorway of a mysterious land.

Those were beautiful days.

At first, Fewee hesitated to go there. The memory of their mother's warning still lived quietly in her mind. But Mira's warm and earnest invitations slowly melted her doubts. Mira had a way of speaking that made everything sound safe and exciting at the same time.

Before long, Fewee and Mira had become the best of friends. They spent hours together, climbing, playing, gathering fruits, and sharing small secrets on quiet branches. Sometimes they laughed so loudly that even the older squirrels nearby turned their heads in amusement.

One morning, without even telling Squee, the two friends decided to visit the mango grove alone. Mira led the way confidently, leaping from branch to branch with effortless grace. Fewee followed closely behind.

Unlike Squee, Mira understood the hidden language of the grove. She knew when the leaves rustled with harmless wind and when they carried danger. She knew where the safest branches were and which paths the predators rarely crossed.

And for the first time, as Fewee followed her into the deeper shade of the mango trees, she felt a strange mixture of excitement and trust. Mira, it seemed, knew exactly how to survive in this world.

As the weeks passed, Mira began to feel a strange change within herself. Nature had its own rhythm, and she had reached the stage of fertility, a feeling she could not explain and could not ignore, an overwhelming urge to nurture life of her own. Among all the squirrels she knew, Squee stood out. He was strong, daring, and full of life. His bravery in the mango grove had never left her mind.

Yet nature had its own laws.

In the wild, a female squirrel could not simply choose freely. When the time came, several males would compete, and the strongest would earn the right to mate. Mira often felt a quiet restlessness about this. In her heart she already knew whom she wished to choose. Still, she hoped.

Finally the day arrived. The instinct within her released a powerful scent into the air, a signal carried on the wind that she was ready. Almost immediately the male squirrels sensed it. One by one they appeared in the branches, drawn by the invisible message.

Squee was among the first.

High above the coconut garden, the great chase began. Mira ran swiftly across the branches, leaping from tree to tree while the males followed behind her. It was the traditional chase of the squirrels, the test that decided strength, speed, and endurance.

The sun slowly moved toward the western sky. One by one, some of the males began to slow down. But Squee did not.

Branch after branch, leap after leap, he followed Mira through the tall coconut trees. His breath came fast, his legs ached, but he did not stop. The chase had become something more than instinct, it felt like a question being asked of him, and he was answering it with every leap.

At last Mira reached the top of a tall tree and paused for a moment on the highest branch. Squee arrived beside her. Both of them stood there breathing quickly in the warm evening air, the whole garden spread golden beneath them.

For a brief moment the world seemed quiet.

Mira looked at him. Among all the squirrels in the grove, he had been the one who kept up with her. Strong. Daring. Relentless.

Slowly, the two moved closer together on the branch. The last light of the afternoon fell warm upon them, and in the gentle stillness that follows a long chase, they drew together, two young creatures following the oldest and most tender law of nature.

But Mira was beautiful, graceful, and admired by many in the grove. And the other males had not yet given up.

Soon the branches began to shake again as the remaining competitors arrived. The moment they saw the pair together, the chase started once more.

Mira sprang away to another tree. Squee followed instantly. The branches blurred beneath him as he leapt through the coconut leaves. Ahead of him, Mira crossed toward a tall tree that stood near an electric pole at the edge of the garden.

Squee gathered all his strength. With a powerful leap, he launched himself through the air.

For a moment it looked perfect.

But fate had written a different ending.

As he flew past the pole, his large bushy tail brushed against a deadly electric wire stretched between the trees.

There was a sudden flash.

For a fraction of a second, the world seemed to freeze. Squee's small body went still. Then he fell silently toward the ground below.

Far beneath the branches, he lay motionless on the quiet earth.

Above, the chase slowly came to an end. One of the other males reached Mira and held her on the branch.

Mira turned just in time to see Squee falling.

Her heart seemed to stop.

For a moment she froze, unable to move. Below her, beneath the fading light of the sunset, Squee lay still among the fallen leaves.

And high above him, life continued in the branches, moving forward, following the ancient and unchanging laws of nature.
